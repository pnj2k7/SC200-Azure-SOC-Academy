Your learning programme

18 days, 12 teaching modules and a final review. Progress is stored in this browser where supported; export it for a durable record. Objective-level evidence remains in the matrix.

Day 01 · Module 01

Open the SOC

A security operations centre is the team that checks warning signs and coordinates a response. Think of it as a hospital control room for digital services.

Day 02 · Module 02

Build the evidence room

A log is a timestamped record of something that happened. Sentinel brings selected records into one place so an analyst can investigate them.

Day 03 · Module 03

Windows tells its story

A workstation can record successful and failed sign-ins, much like a reception access register. We choose which entries to collect so the register remains useful.

Day 04 · Module 03

The missing telemetry

Different systems describe events differently. This lesson makes their records comparable, as if several departments agreed on the same incident form.

Day 05 · Module 04

Protect the reception desk

Endpoint protection watches a computer for suspicious behaviour. Configure its rules and permissions before allowing it to take action.

Day 06 · Module 04

Investigate the workstation

An alert is a warning to investigate. A timeline helps explain what happened before and after the warning; containment limits further harm.

Day 07 · Module 05

Engineer a reliable detection

A detection is a repeatable test for a suspicious pattern. Like a smoke detector, it needs testing: sensitivity that is too high creates noise, while too low can miss danger.

Day 08 · Module 06

Automate with a brake

Automation gives routine tasks to a workflow. Begin with small reversible tasks, such as assigning a case, and keep important decisions under human control.

Day 09 · Module 07

The suspicious invoice

An email’s visible sender name can be misleading. Investigators examine delivery and authentication records and then check what the recipient actually did.

Day 10 · Module 08

Identity crosses the perimeter

An attacker may use a valid account or a connected business application. Changing a password can help, but the investigation also needs to check active access and application permissions.

Day 11 · Module 09

Find the data trail

Finding a file and proving that someone downloaded it are different tasks. Use the right record for each question and collect only the information the investigation needs.

Day 12 · Module 10

Defend the cloud workload

A weak door lock and evidence that someone entered are different findings. Cloud security tools report both weaknesses and suspicious activity; the response must distinguish them.

Day 13 · Module 11

Run the incident bridge

A case connects the warnings, evidence, decisions and people responsible. It should let a colleague understand what is confirmed, what is uncertain and what happens next.

Day 14 · Module 11

Make the SOC observable

A dashboard is useful only when its numbers have a clear meaning. A quiet chart could mean there were no events, or that collection stopped working.

Day 15 · Module 12

Hunt beyond the alert

Hunting starts with a question, such as whether an account behaved unusually, even when no alert has appeared. A good hunt tests both suspicious and harmless explanations.

Day 16 · Module 12

Follow the historical trail

A relationship map shows how accounts, computers and files connect. Historical searches look further back, while summaries reduce detail to make some questions cheaper to answer.

Day 17 · Module 12

Use AI with evidence

AI can help organise an investigation, but a convincing explanation can still be wrong. Ask for evidence and check the underlying records before making a decision.

Day 18 · Module 12

The hospital incident exercise

The final exercise asks you to explain an incident as a responsible colleague would: use evidence, recognise uncertainty, propose proportionate actions and prove that the lab has been closed safely.
