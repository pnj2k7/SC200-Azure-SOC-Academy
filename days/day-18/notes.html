Day 18 notes: The hospital incident exercise

Start in plain language

The final exercise asks you to explain an incident as a responsible colleague would: use evidence, recognise uncertainty, propose proportionate actions and prove that the lab has been closed safely.

Words you will meet

Acceptance criteria: agreed conditions for completion. Residual risk: the risk that remains after action. Sign-off: a recorded decision by the responsible person.

How the parts work together

The capstone tests reasoning under uncertainty, evidence quality and operational discipline. The best outcome is an accurate report with explicit gaps, even if some licensed capabilities remain Pending.

Apply it to Wardlight

An independent analyst must reconstruct the full case and defend a proportionate response before the pilot closes. The technical result matters because the hospital needs a decision it can trust while continuing to serve patients. State what the evidence establishes before recommending an action.

Worked reasoning

Question: When is the capstone ready for sign-off?

Reasoning: When findings are reproducible, response outcomes and recovery are verified, uncertainty and access gaps are explicit, and costs/resources have been checked after cleanup.

Common mistake

Choose two faults from the previous days and diagnose them from evidence without looking at the recorded fixes.

Correction: Repair the root cause, re-run the proof and document the difference between restoring telemetry and remediating compromise.

What the instructor demonstrates

Walk through the first two lab steps, point out the selected tenant/workspace and show a successful result. Explain one failure state and ask learners to predict its cause before correcting it. End by showing the evidence that would allow a colleague to reproduce the finding.
